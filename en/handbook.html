The handbook

One place for
everything you know.

Procedures, house rules and emergency plans in one searchable place instead of scattered across Word, WhatsApp and the shoebox behind the desk. On the phone of whoever needs it, during the shift.

It holds your knowledge, not ours. Everyone reads; writing is for managers and admins.

Who sees what

Not everything is for everyone.

You make your own categories and shield them per role. That matters more than it sounds: a housekeeper looking at forty reception articles also scrolls past the four that are meant for her.

  • A shielded category is not in her list, and Rudy does not quote from it to her
  • You drag articles into the order you want, on a phone too
  • Attachments up to 25 MB. PDFs and photos open in the screen itself

Change a procedure and it is unread again.

Someone confirms per article that they have read it, and that confirmation hangs on the version that stood there at the time. Change the article and it comes back on the list of exactly those people.

Not as a punishment, but because it is true: they read something that is no longer there. And that is the most dangerous kind of not-knowing, because whoever knew the old version will not go back and look on their own.

The old confirmation is kept. That is your proof that they did know the previous version.

Finding it back

The real test is quarter past eleven at night.

A handbook is not a success because it has been written. It is a success when someone finds something in it at quarter past eleven with a guest standing in front of them.

  • Searches inside the text of your attached PDFs too
  • Works on a phone, without a separate app
  • A search with no result points you at a gap in your procedures

Writing

Write it in rumo itself. Not somewhere else.

No Word file to import. You type it straight in, with photos in the text and a video as a link to YouTube or Vimeo.

A photo is not decoration here. A sentence describes a standard, a photo is the standard. That saves you half an hour of discussion per new colleague.

  • That video sits at YouTube, not with us. If someone sets it to private, your procedure is empty without you noticing

Source for Rudy

What you write, Rudy knows.

If someone asks about the way you work, Rudy searches the handbook and adds the article he took it from. He respects the same shielding per role.

If the answer is nowhere, he says so and offers to write an article about it. That is the cheapest signal you will get about a gap in your own procedures.

About rumo itself he answers from the manual. Ask how the calendar works and that comes from us; ask what you do when the fire alarm goes off and that comes from you.
