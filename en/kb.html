Getting started

The first week. Logging in, setting up rooms, booking your first reservation, connecting your first OTA. No two-hour onboarding call. Just do it, and ring us if you get stuck.

First login

Logging in to rumo for the first time

You get a mail from us with a personal link. Click, choose a password, done. No separate hotel ID, no "ask your administrator". If you are the owner yourself, you are the administrator too.

Do not forget: a second factor is a setting of the hotel, not of your own account. A new hotel starts with it required; a hotel from before that default keeps whatever it had. With it on, anyone who does not have one yet is asked for it at their next sign-in.

Setup

Setting up your rooms and room types

Room types first (Standard, Deluxe, Junior Suite, …), then individual rooms. A room type has a rate and a maximum occupancy; an individual room hangs on such a type. The same pattern Booking.com and the other OTAs expect.

Tip: keep room types limited. Three or four is usually enough. Twenty room types gives you twenty synchronisation problems.

Demo data

Playing with made-up reservations

A trial does not start empty but as a finished demo hotel: a few room types, a few rates and a few weeks of bookings on the calendar, so there is something to look at from your first sign-in. Every one of those bookings carries the label Demo; a reservation you make yourself never does, so you cannot mix them up.

There is no button that empties everything in one go. Your own rooms and rates come later, in the conversation with Rudy, at the moment you really start.

Migration

Coming from another PMS

You pull an export out of your current system and read it into your own rumo. Existing reservations, guest profiles and your history come along, and your old system keeps running in the meantime until you switch over. So your file stays with you: we need to receive nothing of it. Most hotels are fully across within a few days, without double entry.

Coming from a dog-eared notebook? That is fine too: then you start with an empty calendar and put the coming bookings in. Just ask and we will go through it together.

Reservations & guest portal

The part you are in every day. Creating, changing, checking in and out, invoicing. Plus the guest portal: the link your guest gets to fill in their own pre-check-in.

Creating

Creating a reservation at the desk

Walk-in, phone, mail: press + New or drag an empty strip on the calendar. Choose room type, dates, number of people. rumo pulls the modifier price for that period automatically; you can adjust it. Guest details can follow later through the pre-check-in link, or straight away by typing them in.

For a walk-in: tick "check in immediately" and the guest is in-house at once and the room status jumps to occupied.

Daily

Checking in and checking out

Checking in: open today's arrivals list. Click a reservation open, check whether the pre-check-in is complete, assign a room if that has not happened yet, status to checked in. Done.

Checking out: open the departures list, add the outstanding items (minibar, breakfasts, extras), take payment, the invoice follows automatically. If something is still open, rumo warns you, but it does not hold you back: checking out with an outstanding balance is allowed, on an invoice to a company for instance.

Groups

A group of several rooms at once

Create one main reservation and add several rooms to it. All under the same booking reference. Dates can differ per room (a wedding where the couple stays one night longer, for instance).

For invoicing you choose: one collective invoice in the organiser's name, or separate invoices per room or guest. Both are possible, without having to enter the booking again.

Changing

Changing, splitting or moving a reservation

Shifting dates or swapping a room is simply a matter of dragging on the calendar. Drag between rooms within the same type, or between types. rumo recalculates the price and proposes a new line. You decide whether it may go through.

Cost items (minibar, breakfasts, extras) can be moved between reservations by searching for a booking reference. No date filter, no restrictions: just find the right reservation and drag the line.

Identity

Scanning an ID or passport at check-in

If the guest did not fill in their details beforehand, you scan their identity card or passport at the desk and name, date of birth and nationality land in the file straight away.

That is the same input as the one from the guest portal, so it also feeds the police registration and the government statistics. Typed once, used three times.

Online check-in

The guest portal for your guest

Every guest gets a personal link in the confirmation mail. With it they fill in themselves:

  • Pre-check-in details (ID, address, date of birth)
  • Confirming or declining a quote
  • Paying online or leaving a card as a guarantee

The link is valid until 30 days after check-out, in case a guest still has to add something or wants to download the invoice.

Cancellations

Cancelling a reservation: direct vs. OTA

Booked directly: cancel in rumo. You decide yourself whether a cancellation fee is charged and whether a credit note is made.

Booked through an OTA: the cancellation has to happen at the OTA (Booking.com, Airbnb, Expedia). rumo synchronises the status automatically as soon as the OTA passes it on. That is a choice by the OTAs, not a shortcoming of rumo. They do not let PMSs write their cancellations back directly.

Quotes

Sending a quote and having it confirmed

Create a reservation with status option, give it an expiry date, and send the guest a link through the guest portal. The guest confirms online and possibly pays right away. Only then does it become a real reservation. No more yes/no/maybe mails.

Calendar

What you can do on the calendar

The calendar shows your rooms one under the other and the days alongside. You choose whether you look at one day, a week or a date range of your own.

Dragging does three things, depending on where to: pulling at the edge extends or shortens a stay, moving to another row moves the booking to another room, and left or right shifts the dates. Every booking also has a pair of scissors to cut a stay in two.

The colours stand for the room status: in house, clean, still to be cleaned, blocked. Click a name and you are in the guest file.

Full but you do not want to lose the request? Then you put it on the waiting list instead of refusing it.

No-show

What to do with a no-show

You mark the reservation as a no-show. rumo keeps the room day in the occupancy statistics (so your revenue is right) and proposes charging the standard no-show fee. Or not: that stays your choice, not the system's.

Guests & companies

Who has stayed before, which company gets the invoice, and which rate goes with it. The file behind your bookings.

Three kinds

Guest, booker or company

Three tabs, and the difference matters. The guest is whoever sleeps there. The booker is whoever made the booking — often the same person, but not with a company or a travel agency. The company is the legal entity that gets the invoice.

Search works per word: type as many as you like, in any order, and every word has to match something on the profile. "maria smit" and "smit maria" both find Maria Smit. It starts searching from two characters, and it answers with at most a hundred matches at a time — see that limit and your search is too broad, not your file too small.

Every profile has a free notes field. That is a memory aid for whoever opens the profile, not a way to select a group of guests: it is not searched and there are no labels.

Price agreements

A fixed rate for a company or a regular guest

A booker and a company can both carry a price agreement: a discount percentage or a fixed nightly rate, as one agreement for everything or per room type. That is what gives a corporate client their negotiated rate by itself, without reception having to remember it or retype it.

If the booker is linked to a company and both have an agreement, the company's wins. Completely. Also for a room type the company left blank and the booker did not. That is deliberate: one change on the company has to reach every booker under it. If the booker's own agreement could still slip in, every rate change would mean checking all its bookers one by one.

A new reservation gets the rate straight away. On an existing one you are asked first whether the prices should be recalculated — otherwise a booking that has already been discussed would quietly change amount.

Your own booking page

A corporate client who books themselves at their rate

A booker can unlock their agreed rate on your own booking page with no account and no password: their email address plus the booker code from their profile is the whole sign-in. It is a lookup, not a session — it holds for that one booking and nothing is remembered afterwards.

The discount is worked out again on the server, for the payment and for the reservation that is written. Nothing comes from the guest's browser, so an agreement cannot be claimed by someone who does not have one.

Three consequences: a promo code does not combine with a negotiated rate — the agreement wins and the code is refused. Remove the agreement while somebody is mid-booking and that booking is refused with an invitation to start again, rather than quietly going through at the public price. And the sign-in fills in only the name and the email address typed: the rest of the profile stays on the server, because a booker code is short and therefore guessable.

Duplicates

The same guest is in there four times

That is normal, not a malfunction. The main cause: OTAs never give you your guest's real address. Booking.com and Airbnb send a fresh relay address with every booking, so a returning guest arrives looking like a stranger every time.

rumo spots those duplicates itself and shows one card per person, not per combination: four profiles of the same Maria Smit are one card saying "Maria Smit · 4 profiles", with the fields that tell them apart. So the counter counts people.

Nothing is ever merged by itself. Two real people can share a name. Merge them yourself and the reservations and invoices move to the profile you keep while the others disappear; anything already checked in keeps the name recorded at the time, because that is the legal register. A merge cannot be undone, so read the confirmation.

Groups & allotments

Holding rooms for a conference, a company or a travel agency. What you hold, what comes back, and who gets the bill.

Setting up

Recording a block of rooms

An allotment holds rooms aside for one party. You record which room type it is and how many of them you hold, between which dates, at which rate, and by when the agreement stops. That last one is the cut-off.

Everyone with the page may read: admin, manager and reception. Creating, changing and deleting is admin and manager. A receptionist sees the whole picture — the blocks, the pickup, the billing — but does not change the contract. That is enforced on the server, so it holds however you reach the screen.

Availability

What an allotment does to your availability

Rooms you hold come off what goes out to the channels and off what your own booking page still offers. Hold five twins with two booked out of the block, and three stay withheld on Booking.com, on the other channels and on your own site.

Three conditions have to hold: the allotment is active, the rooms have not been released yet, and the day falls inside the stay dates. An allotment without dates withholds nothing. Once the block is fully booked it withholds nothing either, because there is nothing left to hold.

One exception: whoever comes through the group's own booking link may use the block up. That is what it is being held for.

Cut-off

What happens at the cut-off

The cut-off is the moment the agreement stops: whatever the group has not booked by then goes back on general sale. Once a day, in the early hours, rumo looks for allotments whose cut-off has passed and that have not been released, and releases them. The rooms reappear in what goes out to the channels, and every release lands in the history.

The cut-off day itself is still yours: the bell shows those allotments so a manager can release earlier. Releasing is one-way — it is recorded once, and a second pass changes nothing.

Facturatie

One invoice for the company, or one per guest

An allotment bills in one of two ways. Individual is the default: every reservation gets its own invoice, like any other booking. Central makes one invoice for the whole contract, in the company's name.

On that central invoice go the rooms of every linked reservation (except cancelled ones and no-shows), the extras marked as centrally billed on their reservation, and the city tax only if you set the scope to everything. That is how you send the room to the company and the minibar to the guest. Every line keeps its own detail — room number, guest name, room type, rate, dates and booking reference — so the company can check the invoice against who slept where.

Watch the payment terms: on central billing they are not enforced. The company is invoiced afterwards, so there is nothing to collect at booking time. Set prepaid there and it stays what you typed and does nothing. If you want money up front from a company, that is an invoice you send and follow up.

Housekeeping & room status

The bridge between what the guest sees and what the team does. The status of every room, the cleaning schedule, blocks for maintenance. All on one screen, usable on a tablet in the corridor too.

Status

The four room statuses

Four core statuses, not fifteen.

  • Dirty: to be cleaned, after a check-out or on request during a stay
  • Clean: ready for the next guest
  • In-house: occupied by a guest
  • Blocked: not available (maintenance, own use)

rumo sets the status to dirty automatically on every check-out. Housekeeping marks clean from the mobile view in the corridor, no separate app.

Planning

A cleaning list for the team

Every morning rumo generates a cleaning list based on check-outs, stay-overs and special requests (extra towels, allergy pillows, early check-in). You do not set the order yourself: the list falls into tabs — departures, stayovers, arrivals and vacant rooms — and that is where the ranking comes from.

The list can be printed or shown on a tablet. Tickable while the team works, with progress visible live at the desk. That way you know which rooms you can give your early guests without ringing first.

Add-on

What Advanced Housekeeping adds

Without the add-on a room has four statuses. With the add-on inspected is added, between clean and ready for the guest. That way nobody checks anyone into a room that has not been looked over.

You also get cleaning checklists that can differ per day, separate extra tasks and a note per room, and a timer that measures how long a clean really takes. That last one comes back in a time report per person and per room.

Switch the add-on off and the basics keep working: clean and dirty do fine without it.

Blocks

Blocking a room for maintenance

Drag a block on the calendar, choose block, and give a reason (leak, renovation, own use). The room is taken out of the pool for OTA sale during that period. You see the block as a striped zone on the calendar.

You block a room yourself on the calendar. Rudy does not line this up for you, but he can explain in plain language where you have to be and what happens then.

Breakfast & F&B

How many covers there are tomorrow, which rooms they belong to, and what the kitchen needs to know about each of them. An overview, not a till.

The count

Which day does a meal belong to?

This is the rule that surprises people, and it follows the stay, not the calendar. Breakfast is the morning after a night: whoever arrives today has breakfast tomorrow, whoever leaves tomorrow still has one that morning. Dinner is the evening of a night, so whoever leaves gets none. Lunch is only for those in house: not on the arrival day and not on the departure day.

One cover is one person, one meal, one day. If the meal sits in the rate plan, every guest in the room counts every day. If it is an extra, the booked quantity is spread over the right days: a breakfast for two of the four mornings occupies exactly those two.

There are three meals: breakfast, lunch and dinner. Which of them your hotel runs you set in the settings. At least one is always on — switching the last one off does not work and falls back to breakfast.

Allergies

What the kitchen has to know about a room

Every room on a reservation can carry F&B labels and a free note. There is no separate diet or allergy field: an allergy is recorded as a label or a note, and it shows up in red on the kitchen list and on the print-out.

A catalogue item can also be marked as a meal note rather than a meal. That gives the kitchen a line ("late breakfast", "no pork") without adding a cover. A room with only notes is on the list all the same, with zero covers.

Ticking off who has been is possible, and two people working the same service see each other's ticks appear without refreshing. It is an aid for the floor and nothing more: it changes no counts, it is not billed and it does not reach the report.

Walk-ins

Somebody sits down unexpectedly

A walk-in here is a guest who is in house and had not booked that meal. You add one from that meal's own tab: pick the room, the number of people, done. It lands as an ordinary billable extra on their reservation, for that one day, and a mistake can be undone straight away.

Two limits. Rooms that already have the meal are not offered, so you cannot double-book anyone. And a restaurant guest who is not staying at the hotel cannot be recorded here: every walk-in hangs on a booking.

The price comes from the first catalogue item ticked for that meal. If that item does not exist, the walk-in cannot be added at all.

Door codes & keys

A numeric code instead of a card. When the guest gets it, what reception sees, and what happens when the network drops. Paid add-on.

The idea

One code per room, not per guest

Each room has one fixed identity in the lock system, and that carries that room's key. rumo never creates or deletes those identities; it only changes the code on them. A guest arrives and the room's code becomes their code. They leave and it is replaced by one nobody knows, which locks them out immediately.

Two consequences. There is one code per room at a time, so two guests of the same booking with two rooms have two different codes. And the lock's own log says "room 101", not the guest's name — rumo knows who held the code at any moment, the lock does not.

This is the Key Management add-on, not a standard part. Without it there is no key icon on a reservation, no tab in the settings and no card in the guest portal. Today rumo works with Salto KS; Dormakaba is announced but not connected yet.

Timing

When the guest gets their code

The code appears when two things are true: the previous guest has been checked out, and housekeeping has set the room to clean or inspected. Not before — a code that exists earlier opens a door with somebody still behind it.

On top of that you can require the guest to check in online first, to pay their balance, or both. Those are guest-portal settings, separate from the settings for checking in and paying themselves. Until then the portal explains what is missing.

The code is never sent by email. An email cannot be taken back and sits in a mailbox for years; a portal link can be withdrawn and the card can close again.

At the desk

What reception sees and can do

On a reservation, every room row has a key icon that opens the door-code panel. The code is there in full, with no extra click — somebody with a guest on the phone should not have to hunt for six digits. If there is no code yet, it says why: the room is still held by another booking (named), the room is being prepared, the arrival day has not come, or the lock system did not answer.

There is a button for a new code. It replaces that room's code immediately, for everyone who has it, even if the guest is already inside — so the guest has to be told the new one. It only works from the booking that currently holds the code; from another reservation it is refused, otherwise you would quietly change the code of whoever is actually in the room.

Opening a door remotely is possible too, and that is recorded in rumo with the name of whoever pressed the button. The lock system itself only ever sees "rumo".

When it fails

No network, no new codes

A digital key is not a replacement for a way in that needs no electricity. Keep a set of tags at reception and keep the mechanical override your installer supplied within reach.

If the connection is gone for longer, the codes issued in the meantime never reached the locks. Issue them again afterwards or hand out tags.

The button in the portal that opens a door needs the phone, rumo, the vendor and the gateway all at once, so it is the first thing to fail. That is why the card always says "not working? use your code" — and if the code does not work either, it is the desk.

Channel manager

How rumo and the OTAs talk to each other, so that one change at your end goes neatly to Booking, Expedia, Airbnb and the rest. Below is what you need to know about it.

How it works

What goes back and forth

Out to the channels go your prices, your availability, your restrictions (min-stay, max-stay, closed on arrival or departure) and the link between your room types and the channel's. Back come new bookings and cancellations, as soon as the OTA reports them.

In practice: you change a price in rumo and within seconds it is on every connected channel. A new booking on Booking.com is in your calendar just as fast. Every change has its own status line: sent, forwarded, arrived.

Setup

Connecting a new OTA

For every channel there is a fixed path: allow rumo as a provider in the OTA's extranet, pass on your property ID, and then link your room types to the channel's in rumo. With Airbnb that goes through a login connection, with Booking through an agreement they have to activate.

We do the first connection together. From the second on it usually goes by itself. Rudy points out the steps, but choices in an OTA's extranet (a Genius promo, a Mobile Rate, your listing text) he leaves to you: those are decisions, not actions.

Sync

When is a change visible at the OTA?

Usually within a few seconds. In busy periods it can take half a minute. If it takes longer, it is down to the channel itself, and you see that in your status log. If something is stuck, you can push it again by hand.

One important nuance: cancellations, no-shows and price changes after booking you do not send back to the OTA from rumo. Most OTAs simply do not accept that type of change. You do it in their extranet, and the status then comes back to rumo by itself.

Commission

Why you get no commission invoice from Expedia

Booking.com is a broker: the guest is your customer and the commission comes afterwards as an invoice. Expedia Collect is a reseller: it keeps its share before the money reaches you, so there is never a commission invoice and there is nothing to deduct.

Put your fixed percentage on that anyway and you deduct that same commission a second time, and the channel looks more expensive than it is.

Conflicts

A double booking, now what?

Theoretically impossible thanks to real-time sync, but in practice it happens once in a while (two OTAs selling the last room at the same time). rumo warns you immediately. You decide which guest you upgrade and which you move, not the algorithms.

Rates & packages

One base rate, and the rest is derived. No entering four different rate plans by hand for one room. No 23 screens to set a weekend price.

Base model

How rumo models prices

You start from a base rate per room type. On top come modifiers: occupancy (1, 2, 3 people), child price, length of stay, channel uplift, season. You adjust the modifier, not the four derived rates.

That is different from most channel managers, where you have to maintain a separate line for every occupancy. We think that is unnecessary work.

Occupancy

Rates per occupancy (1, 2, 3 people)

For a room where 1, 2 or 3 people can stay: set one base rate and two modifiers. For example −€30 for single occupancy and +€40 for the third guest. rumo works out the three rates and sends them all to the OTAs.

Packages

Building a package (room + breakfast + dinner)

A package is a rate with extras included. "Romantic weekend" = room + breakfast + four-course menu, for one price. On the invoice the three items are split out neatly (handy for VAT); on the OTA rate guests see one amount.

Periods

Season and weekend prices

Select a date range on the calendar, choose the modifier ("+15% for the summer season"), done. Works on all room types at once or on one. Can also be per weekday: a different price on Friday/Saturday than on Monday/Tuesday.

Worked example

How a price on Booking.com comes about

Concretely, with figures. Junior Suite, base rate €140 per night for 2 people. A guest books 2 nights Saturday-Sunday, with 3 people, through Booking.com:

  • Base rate: €140
  • + Weekend (+10%): €154
  • + Third person (+€40): €194
  • + OTA uplift Booking (+12%): €217
  • × 2 nights: €434

That is what the guest sees on Booking. rumo shows you the same stacking when you hover over a price in the calendar. So you always know why a rate is what it is, and which modifier to adjust if you want to change it.

Invoicing & Peppol

Accounting-correct invoicing without you having to think about it. VAT split per line, every invoice as UBL or PDF, and Peppol sending for B2B through the Peppol add-on.

Peppol

How Peppol works in rumo

Compulsory for B2B invoices in Belgium from 2026. As standard, rumo turns every invoice into a UBL file or a PDF you can download and forward. Automatically sending over the Peppol network, to your customer's Peppol ID, is in the Peppol add-on. With that add-on on it happens without a separate tool and without manual sending.

Private individuals simply get a PDF by mail. rumo recognises the difference and does the right thing.

VAT

VAT split 6% hospitality / 21%

The right rate per invoice line: room and breakfast at 6%, alcohol and other services at 21%. With packages the split is applied automatically to the composed lines.

At the end of the month or the quarter you export a VAT report for your accountant. With base, VAT amount and totals per rate.

Credit notes

Making a credit note

Invoices in rumo cannot be deleted; nor should they be, and that is exactly what your accountant likes. Want to correct something? Make a credit note. rumo automatically links it to the original invoice. Audit trail complete.

Documents

Which document rumo picks itself

rumo makes four kinds of document: an invoice, a proforma, a credit note and a receipt for private individuals. You do not have to work out which type it becomes: that follows from who pays and whether a company number is known.

A credit note always hangs on the invoice it corrects, so that you can see later which two belong together.

Lines of the bill can be moved to another reservation. That is what you need with a group where the organiser pays the rooms and every guest their own consumptions.

Boekhouding

Passing on to Octopus, Exact Online, Yuki

A direct connection with the three most used accounting packages in BE/NL. Invoices, credit notes and payments land in your bookkeeping automatically. From now on your accountant gets fewer mails from you, and that is pleasant for both of you.

Paying

Which payment methods rumo supports

  • Stripe for card and Bancontact, both online (through a payment link) and on a connected card terminal. Stripe also covers keeping a card on file and charging it later.
  • Cash you register manually as a cash payment on the booking
  • Bank transfer you register manually, and you confirm it as soon as the money is actually in your account
  • Pre-authorisation (card guarantee without charging right away) through Stripe, for late arrivals or OTA bookings without a deposit

Every payment lands on the booking immediately and is settled automatically against the right invoice line. No more manual matching.

Deposits

Deposits and partial invoices

You collect a deposit through a payment link (Stripe) or register it manually after a transfer. rumo puts it on the booking as "received, still to be settled". At check-out it is deducted from the final amount automatically.

For longer stays, packages or groups you can send partial invoices (50% on booking, 50% on arrival, for instance). Every partial invoice is a real invoice, with its own number and its own Peppol sending. At check-out the final invoice is drawn up for the remaining balance.

Statistics & reports

The figures you base decisions on. Occupancy, revenue, channel mix, comparison with last year. Clickable down to reservation level, exportable for your accountant or for your own analysis.

KPIs

Reading occupancy, ADR and RevPAR

Three figures every hotelier should know. rumo shows them per day, week, month, year, with a comparison to the same period last year.

  • Occupancy: per day, the occupied rooms divided by your room count. Over a period the figure is the average of those days, not one big division — that is the difference if you recompute it by hand
  • ADR (Average Daily Rate): room revenue / nights sold
  • RevPAR (Revenue per Available Room): occupancy × ADR, or room revenue per available room night

Hover over a figure for the arithmetic. Click to drill down to the individual reservations that make it up.

Rapporten

Running monthly and yearly reports

One click on period report gives you: revenue per channel, revenue per room type, occupancy per day, daily cash flow, VAT split per rate, average length of stay. Export as PDF for your insurer or accountant, or as XLS for further analysis in Excel or Google Sheets.

For the monthly close: one page you can print, sign and put in the cupboard for the tax office. No more manual summaries.

Pace

Your booking curve against last year

Pace puts side by side how much you had already booked on this day last year and how much now. That says something different from your occupancy: it is about the speed at which a period fills up, not about how full it eventually gets.

Next to Pace there are tabs for the nationality of your guests, your invoices and your online check-ins. Who still has to pay is no longer with the reports but under Payments → Debtors, next to the money that did come in.

Everything can be exported to CSV, Excel or PDF.

Channel mix

Which OTA brings you the most net?

Per channel: number of bookings, average rate, average length of stay and the share of cancellations. Plus the commission, which at Booking and Airbnb comes along with the booking and therefore does not have to be estimated. So you see net yield per channel, not only gross revenue.

Often an eye-opener: an OTA with a lot of volume but 22% commission sometimes yields less than your own website at 0%. With that figure in hand you can decide where your marketing euro lands best.

Belgian compliance

The part where most international PMSs drop the ball. Municipal tax, police registration, the Brussels accommodation levy. We do it, because we live here.

City tax

Municipal tax / city tax

Your municipality's rule you set up once in the settings: a fixed amount per guest per night or a percentage of the room price, with the VAT rate it carries and who the regulation names as liable. After that rumo applies it to every reservation by itself, visible as its own line on the invoice.

rumo does not know the regulations of the Belgian municipalities on its own, and that is deliberate: they differ per municipality and per year, and a table we maintained would quietly go stale. If the rate changes, you add the new value with the date it takes effect — which rate a booking gets follows from its arrival date, not from when it was booked.

Children and babies do not count: the tax only charges adults. Anyone booked as a child pays nothing, and that is the age exemption you see in most regulations.

For the declaration the report is ready under Reports → Compliance, over the period you pick.

Police registration

Guest registration for the police

Since the law on guest registration you are obliged to record a number of details for every guest: name, date of birth, ID number, address. The guest portal asks your guest for those details during the pre-check-in, before they arrive. You have to type nothing.

The export to the police is in your rumo. Monthly or on request, in the right format.

Statbel

Monthly FPS Statbel declaration

Belgium requires a monthly declaration for all commercial accommodation above a certain size. rumo automatically counts overnight stays per nationality, average length of stay, number of arrivals and the number of guests. One click and the declaration is ready in the Statbel format.

Filing stays your responsibility. But the counting, the right format, the split per country of origin: that has already happened.

VAT return

What rumo does for your VAT return

Honestly: rumo does not file your VAT return itself and does not make a ready-made return file for it either. That stays the work of you or your accountant. What rumo does do is keep the basis right: every invoice line gets the correct rate (6% accommodation, 21% for the rest), and the figures and invoices are ready to hand over.

If you work with the Scrada add-on, your invoices and payments land in your bookkeeping automatically, which is where the return starts. Without that connection you pass the PDFs or the UBL invoices to your accountant. In both cases the rates are already split correctly, so there is nothing left to untangle.

Privacy & data

Where your guest data sits, for how long, and who can get at it. Questions your guest is going to ask you one day, and that the GDPR supervisor already wants answered.

Hosting

Where is my guest data

rumo runs on servers inside the EU (Frankfurt). No American cloud, no data transfer outside the EEA. GDPR-compliant by design, not as an afterthought.

Backups are encrypted and sit at a second EU location. Should the main data centre be unreachable, your rumo is back within the hour, with data at most five minutes old.

Retention

How long data is kept

  • Invoice and accounting data: 7 years (Belgian VAT Code)
  • Guest registration data: according to police regulations, usually 5 years
  • Marketing consent: until withdrawn by the guest
  • Other personal data: up to 2 years after the last stay

Those windows come from the law and from your own privacy statement; they are not something rumo enforces on guest data today. What is cleaned up automatically is what you set yourself: your log, your price changes and your sign-ins (a year by default), your channel activity (90 days by default) and your internal team messages. That purge runs every night and leaves a line in the log saying how much went.

GDPR

A guest asks for their data to be erased

Honestly: there is no button today that anonymises one guest in one click. A guest profile with no bookings can be deleted, and then it really is gone. If a reservation hangs on it, rumo refuses the deletion — an invoice and a police registration may not simply disappear, and cutting a profile loose from its bookings would do exactly that.

What does exist, and what answers most of the questions: guest data is shielded by default, every time somebody clicks it open that lands in the log, and housekeeping and F&B are not sent it at all. If you get a real erasure request on a guest with history, pass it to us — for now that is handwork, not a self-service button.

Handbook & announcements

Your own procedures in one searchable place, and short notices that disappear by themselves. Everyone reads; writing is for managers and admins.

Categories

Not everything is for everyone

Articles live in categories, and a category can be shielded per role. A "Reception" category open only to receptionists does not appear for housekeeping — and Rudy does not quote from it to them either. A category with no shielding is for everyone. Admins and managers always see everything.

That matters more than it sounds: a housekeeper looking at forty reception articles also scrolls past the four that are meant for her.

Deleting a category deletes everything in it, articles and attachments included. The order inside a category is yours to set, on a phone too.

Read

Change a procedure and it is unread again

Somebody confirms per article that they have read it, and that confirmation hangs on the version that stood there at the time. Change the article and it comes back on the list of exactly those people.

Not as a punishment, but because it is true: they read something that is no longer there. And that is the most dangerous kind of not-knowing, because whoever knew the old version will not go back and look on their own.

The old confirmation is kept. That is your proof that they did know the previous version, and that is exactly what you want to be able to show when something ever happens.

Writing

Photos, attachments and a video

You type an article straight into rumo. A photo goes in the text or hangs on it as an attachment; PDFs and images open in the screen itself, up to 25 MB each.

A photo is not decoration here. A sentence describes a standard, a photo is the standard. That saves you half an hour of discussion per new colleague.

A video you add as a link to YouTube or Vimeo, and it plays in the article itself. Uploading a video file is not possible, and that has one consequence you should know: the video sits at YouTube, not in rumo. If somebody sets it to private, your procedure is empty without you noticing.

Announcements

The department decides who sees it

Next to the handbook sit announcements: a note to the team rather than a procedure to look up. Each has a department and optionally an expiry date, and an expired announcement disappears by itself — it stays through its expiry day and is gone the day after.

The department is not a label but the audience. Pick Housekeeping and housekeeping sees it and nobody else. Managers and admins always see everything, whatever was chosen: they write them. If there is a department rumo does not know — an old announcement from before — it goes to everyone rather than to nobody.

Announcements work on the same principle as articles: they stay in the bell until confirmed, and an edited announcement comes back.

Prevention & safety

Inspections, certificates, registers and who came to work in your building. What expires, where the paper is, and what you did. Paid add-on.

Installations

The status follows from the expiry date

Per installation you record what it is, how many there are, when it was last inspected, until when the current certificate is valid, who maintains it and who inspects it. Those last two are deliberately separate names, because that is the question you ask a year from now.

You do not set the status yourself: expired, within thirty days, or in order — that follows from the date. An installation without a date gets no status, and that is not the same as being in order: it is simply never flagged. So fill one in even when you have to estimate.

rumo does not know the statutory interval for your installation. What you fill in is what it works with, and you take that from the certificate in your hand. An expired inspection blocks nothing in rumo — the warning and the paper trail are the value; what it really costs you sits with your insurer.

Tasks

A task remembers the date, a checklist leaves proof

Tasks are the recurring routines: check the emergency lighting, test the fire alarm, flush the rarely used taps. Each task has a frequency and a next due date; ticking one off sets today as the last execution and moves the next one on.

Two things to know. Monthly means thirty days and annually 365 — not calendar months, so a task done on the 31st drifts. And a task keeps only its most recent execution: there is no history of past rounds.

If you want to be able to show that you did it, use a checklist. That leaves a trail: who carried it out, what came out per point, and a signature. A finished round becomes a PDF with your documents by itself, and that is what makes it usable as evidence.

Contractors

Who came to work in your building

Contractors do not have to be typed in by reception. The visit register carries a sign-in link with a QR code: hang it at the service entrance or send it with the work order, and the contractor registers themselves without a rumo login.

They fill in their name and company, phone, licence plate, when they arrive and leave, what kind of work and where, who their contact is, and which risk classes apply. They also state whether a work permit is needed and whether they were given a master key — one that opens every room. Then they sign for the safety instructions, on screen.

Those visit records are deleted automatically after two years. That is a privacy measure and it is not configurable, so export anything you want to keep before then.

Warnings

Thirty, fourteen, seven and one day

A nightly job looks at the expiry dates of your documents and warns thirty, fourteen, seven and one day ahead. It runs in the early morning, on your hotel's own clock.

The bell is worked out live when you open it: installations expiring within thirty days, tasks that are overdue, checklists that are due, an evacuation drill older than a year, open incidents, and documents waiting in the queue. The bell is for managers and admins.

Three honest limitations. These warnings are in the app only: no email and no push notification goes out for an expiring certificate, so somebody has to open rumo. The nightly job only watches documents. And the installations counter has no lower bound, so something that expired two years ago keeps being counted under "soon" — read that number as "needs attention".

The training hotel

A practice copy of your own hotel, with made-up guests and the wires to the outside cut. Free, and it does not count as a second hotel.

What is switched off

What cannot get out

That is the first question to ask about any practice environment, and here the answer is four things. Mail to guests is blocked: no confirmation, no invoice, no pre-check-in link. You can set the mails to go to a short list of your own colleagues so you see them arrive; "everyone" is not an option in a training hotel.

The channel manager cannot be connected, so no availability and no price ever leaves for Booking.com, and no OTA booking comes in. Payments do not move: the screens, the payment links and the invoices all work, only the money stays put. And the door locks are not carried over, so a practice check-in never opens a real door.

Your own web address does not come along either, and the public booking page is closed by default. The guest portal does stay open, so the whole guest flow can be rehearsed.

Practising

Rudy gives the assignment, the database marks it

Training is a conversation with Rudy. He proposes one exercise, says in two sentences why it matters, and gives the assignment. You do it in the app, not in the chat. Then he looks in the data to see whether it is really there.

He looks at the result, not at your clicks. An exercise is finished when the situation in rumo is right: the booking is checked in, the bill is at zero, the credit note exists. How you got there is your business, and there is usually more than one right route. Rudy does not decide whether you passed either: he asks the database and reads back what it said. He cannot tick off work that is not there, and he cannot refuse work that is.

A few exercises are on your honour — reading the colour legend, moving around the calendar, walking through the guest portal. Those leave nothing in the data, so there is nothing to look up. Rudy says so plainly rather than pretending to check.

What you get

You only see what applies to you

The course falls into four chapters: reception, the guest portal, housekeeping and F&B. Which you see depends on your role — a housekeeper gets the cleaning chapter and a receptionist does not, and somebody with both gets both. The exercises are also filtered by the add-ons your hotel has and by what is actually configured.

An exercise never names a room number or a rate: those differ per hotel. It says "today's arrival", and rumo picks a real one from the practice data when the exercise starts. If there is nothing suitable in there, it says so rather than sending you after something that cannot be done.

Skipping is always allowed, anything can be done again, and stopping halfway is fine: come back later and Rudy picks up where you left off. Your progress is your own — nobody else sees it, managers included. No score, no certificate, no report. And the assignments come in your language: Dutch, French or English.

Users & roles

Who may do what in rumo. Four roles, not fifty tick boxes. Enough to give a receptionist, housekeeping and an accountant each their own rumo, without an afternoon of explaining.

Roles

The roles in rumo

  • Admin and manager: everything. Rates, invoicing, reports, settings, user management, connections. Today those two can do exactly the same; they are kept apart for the day they diverge
  • Receptionist: the daily work. Reservations, checking in and out, invoicing and payments, the calendar, the guest profiles, and in the channel manager the everyday moves like a stop-sell or a price for a date. No reports, no settings, no user management, and the revenue figures stay out of sight
  • Housekeeping: the cleaning screen, issues, the handbook, the training hotel and support. No prices, and guest data does not even reach this screen
  • F&B: the same five screens, with the breakfast screen in place of housekeeping

No 47 tick boxes to combine. One thing that surprises people: a receptionist may touch the prices. That is deliberate — they are the one at the desk when it has to be decided. Want it otherwise? Send us a message.

Inviting

Inviting a colleague

Settings → users → + invite. Enter an email, pick one or more roles. Your colleague gets a mail and chooses their own password or sets a passkey. If the second factor is on at your hotel, they are asked for one at their first sign-in. Withdrawing access again is one click.

There is no expiry date per user: somebody who no longer belongs there you set to inactive or remove. There is no separate accountant role either — give them the access they need and take it away when the work is done.

Audit

Who did what: the audit log

Every change that matters (cancellation, price adjustment, refund, user creation, invoice change) is logged with the time, the user and the old/new value. Unchangeable, not even by the owner.

Handy for internal questions ("who gave that discount?"), fraud checks, or when the accountant wants to trace something. And it protects you against false claims, because you can always look back.

Settings & your subscription

Where your hotel is set up, how saving works, and what happens when you switch an add-on on or off.

Saving

What happens when you save

Settings is for managers and admins; reception, housekeeping and F&B do not see it. Your edits stay on your screen until you save: a bar appears at the bottom the moment something is unsaved, and moving away with unsaved work asks first.

Three things that protect your configuration. Only what you touched is written, so two people can work in different parts without erasing each other. The confirmation waits for the server: only once it has accepted does it say saved. And if a colleague — or Rudy — worked in the same list meanwhile, saving on top of a change you never saw is refused, with a message saying what happened.

One category deserves extra care: lists that are replaced as a whole. Room types, rate plans, cancellation policies, extras, email templates and the channel manager's rates. Saving there means: this list is the list from now on, so anything missing from it is gone.

City tax

A rate that changes next month

City tax you set up under the general details: on or off, what it is called, a fixed amount per guest per night or a percentage of the room price, which VAT rate it carries, and whether it appears as its own line on the invoice.

If your municipality changes the amount, you do not have to wait until it applies: you add a scheduled change with the date it takes effect, as many as you need. Which rate a booking gets follows from its arrival date, not from when it was booked. Enter January's increase in November and every stay from January picks it up, including bookings already in the house.

The amount is never frozen onto a booking: it is recalculated wherever it is shown, invoiced or charged. So a rate change reaches every stay that has not been invoiced yet.

Add-ons

Trying an add-on for five days

Five add-ons can be started as a free five-day trial instead of paying for them straight away: Prevention, Upsell, Advanced Housekeeping, Smart Pricing and the OTA inbox. Switching one on starts the trial directly; there is no separate button to skip it.

If nothing else happens, the add-on switches itself off after five days, with no charge. About a day before that, admins and managers get a push notification in case it has been forgotten. There is a button to confirm the add-on right away, but none to stop early: not wanting it after all means letting it run out. Each trial is a one-time offer per hotel.

Three add-ons have none. White Label and Key Management do not, because a trial ending while guests are in house does not hide a tab but takes down a booking domain or a door key in use at that moment. Peppol and Full Scrada do not, because switching either on immediately sends something real to the outside world — an invoice on the Peppol network or a booking into your accounts — that you do not take back when the trial ends.

Trial

What happens when your trial runs out

A hotel on trial is a complete rumo: nothing is switched off and nothing is crippled, and everything you set up during the trial is kept if you become a customer. The end date can be moved in either direction — an extra week is a question, and stopping earlier is possible too.

Once that date passes, the hotel goes read-only rather than dark: everyone can still log in and read everything — reservations, invoices, guests — but nothing new is created and nothing changes. That lasts fourteen days. It is your window to decide, to get your data out, or to become a customer. After it, the hotel closes.

Troubleshooting

When something does not work. Not "open a ticket and wait three days", but "click here, look there, and if it is still odd you ring us".

Sync

My price change is not on Booking

Three checks, in this order:

  1. rumo status log: has the change left? If not, it is at your end (browser conflict, not saved)
  2. Sending status: has the change been forwarded? If not, it is in the connection itself (mapping or access)
  3. Booking extranet: arrived? If so, all is well and it is in the OTA cache (a few minutes' patience)

Click the change in the status log and you get the exact error: mapping not found, OTA token expired, price below min rate, or something else. Not solved in five minutes? Send a WhatsApp and we will look straight away.

City tax

The city tax on the invoice is wrong

The three most common causes:

  • A guest is booked as an adult while it is a child. The tax counts adults only, so that one wrong category is a night too many straight away
  • A cancellation or no-show still counting. Whoever did not sleep there owes no tax — check the status of the room, not of the booking
  • The rate in your settings is no longer your municipality’s rate

Open the booking and click the tax line: you get the calculation, per room and per night. Is the rate itself out of date? You change that yourself in the settings — with the date the new one takes effect, so bookings from before stay as they were. We keep no list of municipal rates, so there is nothing for you to wait on us for.

Correction

I have charged a wrong night or price

Before the invoice has been sent: open the booking, adjust the price line, rumo recalculates automatically. The change is in the audit log with the old and the new value.

After the invoice: make a credit note for the wrong amount and then a new invoice for the right amount. Both hang together in the audit trail; your accountant sees the correction neatly without you having to explain anything.

OTA

The connection with an OTA is broken

Symptom: new bookings do not come in, prices do not synchronise, or you get a red warning in rumo. Open settings → channels and look at the coloured dot next to each OTA.

Red = connection broken (often an expired token or a credential changed in the OTA extranet). Click the OTA, choose reconnect. With OAuth channels (Airbnb, some Booking set-ups) you have to log in to the OTA again and authorise. Two minutes' work, no support ticket needed.

Status

rumo is slow or unreachable

First reflex: check status.rumo.eu. That is where we see outages before we report them. No outage visible but it does not work for you? Hard refresh (Ctrl+Shift+R / Cmd+Shift+R), another tab, another browser, another network connection.

Still not working? Send us a WhatsApp with a screenshot of what you see. No queue, no ticket system, just a message and we will look.

What Rudy can do, in one paragraph

Rudy reads these articles, your handbook and your documents, and knows rumo from the inside. What he does

What Rudy concretely does for you

A defined list, not a magic wand. Concretely:

  • Creating a reservation (guest, room, dates)
  • Making a quote out of a request mail: he lines it up as an option with a confirmation link for the guest, and drafts a reply mail with it
  • Filling a pasted group or rooming list into the right rooms (a preview first; only after your OK does he write)

What he does not do

Two kinds. First, things he could do but deliberately leaves alone, because it is not work for an assistant:

Second, things he simply cannot do, and that you therefore do yourself:

  • No talking to the guest directly: he drafts an answer, you press send
  • No actions in the OTA extranets (Genius promo, Mobile Rate, listing content)

The idea: Rudy does the time-consuming preparation, you keep the buttons that matter.
