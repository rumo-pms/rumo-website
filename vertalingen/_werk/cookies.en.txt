9|What is a cookie?
10|A cookie is a small file a website stores in your browser so that it can recognise you later. Some are needed to make the site work (for example: remembering that you are logged in). Others are there to follow your behaviour for advertising.
11|<strong>We only use the first kind.</strong>
12|Which cookies (and cookie-like things) we set
13|Strictly speaking we do not use classic browser cookies, but we do use a few items in <code>localStorage</code>. That is technically the same story, a small file in your browser, and it falls under the same rules.

30|Visitor statistics on this website
31|On <strong>rumo.eu</strong> (the website you are on now, not the software itself) we count how many people come by. We do that with <strong>Plausible</strong>, a European counter that needs no cookie for it and leaves nothing in your browser. So there is no extra row in the table above, because there is nothing to add.
32|What we see of it: which pages are viewed, which site someone arrives from, from which country, and whether it is a phone or a laptop. All added together, never per person. No profile of you is built, not on this site and certainly not beyond it. Come back tomorrow and the counter does not know it is you.
33|We also count a handful of <strong>actions</strong>: that someone opens the trial form, that they send it, or that sending failed. Without that last one we would not notice a request disappearing on the way. Nothing there is tied to a person either; we only see numbers.
34|Because nothing is stored on your device, no consent banner is needed for this. And because it is a form of measuring all the same, it is written out here rather than tucked into a footnote.
35|What we do <em>not</em> do
36|No Google Analytics and no Google Tag Manager. Just the cookieless counter above, and nothing else
37|No Facebook Pixel, no LinkedIn Insight, no TikTok pixel
38|No Hotjar, Mixpanel, Segment or other session-replay tools
39|No advertising cookies, no retargeting, no cross-site tracking
40|No sale or passing on of browsing behaviour to third parties
41|If that ever changes (because a hotel wants its own booking pixel, for instance), a real cookie banner will appear with an accept and a refuse button. Until then: not needed.
