68|More than sync
69|What else rumo <em>does</em> with the OTAs.
70|Sending rates and availability is the beginning. Behind the scenes rumo settles a good deal more that you would otherwise have to sort out by hand at Booking or Airbnb.
71|Booking.com
72|Invalid card? <em>Booking</em> sorts it out.
73|If a guest card turns out to be dead, rumo reports that back to Booking.com. Booking asks the guest for a new card and gives you the right to cancel if it does not work out.
74|Inbox + Rudy
75|Guest messages from the OTAs, <em>in one place</em>.
76|Messages from Booking.com and Airbnb arrive in rumo, Rudy drafts the answer and reception sends it. Expedia does not take part in this. You take this connection separately; the rest of this page is in your package.
77|Virtual cards
78|Card handling <em>per channel</em>, automatically.
79|Verify, pre-authorise or charge automatically, set per channel. A virtual card from Booking often only becomes valid shortly before arrival, so rumo does not try too early and retries by itself.
80|Net revenue
81|The <em>real</em> commission, per booking.
82|rumo reads the commission amount that comes along with every booking. So your net revenue per reservation is right, instead of guesswork with an average percentage.
83|Optional add-on
84|<em>Smart Pricing</em>, built in.
85|Prices that move with your occupancy, in steps you set yourself and pushed straight to your channels. No external tool needed.
86|Deduplication
87|No <em>double</em> imports.
88|If a channel sends the same booking twice, rumo recognises it and holds the duplicate back.
